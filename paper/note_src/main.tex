\begin{abstract}
\noindent \textbf{HYPOTHESIS\_v4 is our live hypothesis: high risk, high reward, and not yet tested.} A passive maker on polymarket.com football joins the best bid and ask (Arm A always; Arm B only while the spread is at least \val{v4_armb_c} cents), frozen at \val{v4_frozen_et} (\texttt{\val{v4_commit}}) before any test data. Its origin is one simulated day on recorded top of book (Oct 3, \val{v4_games} games, maker fee 0): +\$\val{v4_pnl60_usd} at +60 s, \val{mmN_pnl60_c} cents per contract \val{mmN_pnl60_ci}, excluding the five largest games the estimate stays positive at +\val{mmN_pnl60_excl5_c} cents per contract \val{v4_excl5_ci} (condition (2) of v4's own decision rule), and \val{v4_share_pos_59} of the \val{v4_games_with_fills} games with fills were positive (\val{v4_share_pos_88} of all \val{v4_games}). Under our reading rule (games with fills) all four conditions pass: the +60 s interval excludes zero, so we treat it as a real lead that needs more testing on live games. The confirmatory test runs once on Oct 8 to 12 games. v4 grew out of an earlier pre-registered Kalshi study (the v3 strategies, not v4) that did not survive its single holdout test, after which \val{led_grand_total} disclosed trials found no other edge. Taking liquidity does not pay after costs, so v4 crosses into largely uncharted territory: is a patient maker paid for supplying liquidity to recreational sports flow? The reward is an edge that grows with the spread; the risk is one day of evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{The live hypothesis: HYPOTHESIS\_v4 (high risk, high reward)}

\textbf{Rule (frozen, \texttt{\val{v4_commit}}).} On polymarket.com (and, for the confirmatory test, Polymarket US through its authenticated order-book websocket), quote both sides at the best bid and ask, joining and never improving, \val{spec_qty} contracts, inventory cap $\pm$\val{v4_cap}, no Kalshi signal. Arm A always quotes; Arm B quotes only while the spread is at least \val{v4_armb_c} cents. Fills only on strict trade-through; published maker fees; kickoff window cut. Primary metric: P\&L per contract marked to mid at +60 s, game-bootstrap 95\% CI (seed \val{v4_seed}). \textbf{Economic logic:} our earlier lead-lag test (Section~\ref{sec:v3}) found no quote-level lag between Kalshi and polymarket.com, so a resting quote is rarely stale when hit; a maker is paid the spread for providing liquidity to recreational flow, and the edge persists if spread capture exceeds adverse selection \cite{burgi2025}.

\textbf{Origin evidence (descriptive, one day, not pre-registered, simulated on recorded top of book, maker fee 0, queue position unknown).} The plain maker on Oct 3 (\val{v4_games} games, \val{v4_games_with_fills} with fills, \val{v4_fills} fills) made +\$\val{v4_pnl60_usd} at +60 s and +\$\val{v4_pnlset_usd} held to settlement; \val{mmN_pnl60_c} cents per contract \val{mmN_pnl60_ci} at +60 s and \val{v4_set_c} cents \val{mmN_set_ci} at settlement. It captured \$\val{v4_spread_usd} of spread (\val{mmN_spread_c} cents per contract) and lost \$\val{v4_adverse_usd} (\val{mmN_adverse_c} cents) to adverse selection. It loses at 1 cent spreads (\val{mmN_1c_c} cents) and wins at 3 cents or more (+\val{v4_3c_c}), hence Arm B. Per-game Sharpe (mean over standard deviation of game P\&L, not annualized) is \val{v4_sr_game_60} \val{v4_sr_game_60_ci} at +60 s and \val{v4_sr_game_set} \val{v4_sr_game_set_ci} at settlement; one day supports no annualized figure. Five games carry \$\val{mmN_top5_usd} of the \$\val{mmN_all_usd} (Figure~\ref{fig:v4}); excluding the five largest games, the estimate stays positive at +\val{mmN_pnl60_excl5_c} c per contract \val{v4_excl5_ci}; this is condition (2) of v4's own decision rule. \val{v4_share_pos_59} of the \val{v4_games_with_fills} games with fills were positive (\val{v4_share_pos_88} of all \val{v4_games}). Our ruling: under the reading rule applied to the \val{v4_games_with_fills} games with fills, all four conditions pass (interval above zero, positive without the top five games, a majority of games positive, break-even maker fee above 0.5 cents), so this is a lead worth testing live. The rule was written after these numbers were seen; read over all 88 games (38.6\% positive) its game-share condition fails, which is why the live test decides. \textbf{Disclosure:} the origin run used Oct 3 and 4 games, inside the holdout period, with no committed authorization; it can motivate v4, never test it.

\textbf{Why v4 is not checked on the earlier holdout.} For Aug to Oct 2 we hold only polymarket.com taker trades at 1 s resolution, no order books, so the join and trade-through rule cannot be simulated faithfully. \textbf{Test plan.} Test set 1 had 0 eligible games (recordings ended before the late Oct 3 games finished). Test set 2 (confirmatory) is every college and NFL game from Oct 8 00:00 ET to Oct 13 00:00 ET, run once after the window closes. Decision, per venue and arm with Holm across venues: supported only if (1) the primary CI is above zero, (2) the point estimate excluding the top five games is above zero, and (3) the settlement P\&L point estimate is above zero; otherwise not supported, reported either way.

\section{Before the plot twist: the pre-registered v3 study (not v4)}\label{sec:v3}

\textbf{Nothing in this part is v4; v4 has no result yet.} This is the earlier pre-registered study that led to v4. Its strategies (the v3 strategies) did not survive their single holdout test, and \val{led_grand_total} disclosed post-hoc trials found no other edge (lowest Reality Check $p$ \val{v4_rc_min_p} over \val{v4_rc_min_n}). The passive maker is the one idea that survived a reading, and it was frozen as v4 before any test data.

\subsection{Economic hypothesis}

A first CME versus Kalshi hypothesis was retired before any backtest (no CME NFL game in the holdout window). Before v4, we pre-registered two hypotheses and three Kalshi strategies (the v3 strategies A, A-maker and B; unrelated to v4's Arms A and B). \textbf{H1, lead-lag:} one venue's book reprices before another's over 1 to 15 s because participants and order handling differ (polymarket.com applies a taker delay and cancels resting sports orders at game start) \cite{hasbrouck1995,wolfers2004}. \textbf{H2, favorite-longshot bias:} bettors overpay for longshots \cite{thaler1988,snowberg2010,burgi2025}. Strategy \textbf{A} buys the pregame favorite as a taker, \textbf{A-maker} rests the same order, and \textbf{B} trades Kalshi toward polymarket.com when the two disagree for several seconds. Every amendment is a dated commit before the single holdout run (\texttt{\val{run_sha}}).

\subsection{Data and universe}

\textbf{Kalshi:} historical trades for every KXNFLGAME and KXNCAAFGAME market (training \val{training_season_first_day} to \val{training_season_last_day}) and live websocket books for the holdout; Webull routes retail orders to it, and its fee is our primary cost. \textbf{polymarket.com:} trades for training, live books for the holdout (US persons cannot trade it; we only read its data). \textbf{Polymarket US:} polled quotes and time-and-sales, holdout only. ESPN kickoffs; two recorders never spliced within a game; every listed game kept, voids excluded and listed, ties settle at 0.5. An orientation gate checked that each game's two Kalshi team markets sum to about one (n \val{OOS_orientation_gate_n}, \val{OOS_orientation_gate_n_outside} outside the band).

\subsection{Methodology}

\textbf{No lookahead.} A decision at $t$ uses only rows at or before $t$ (as-of joins); fills happen at $t$ plus \val{spec_latency_s} s, never at $t$. \textbf{Sealed holdout.} Games with kickoff on or after \val{spec_seal_date} were never loaded before the run; the run script refuses them otherwise. \textbf{Costs.} Webull \$\val{spec_webull_fee} per contract per fill (primary) and Kalshi direct fees, plus a half-spread; every result is also shown at costs $\times 2$; \val{spec_qty} contracts per order, never optimized. \textbf{Lead test.} Per game, the lag maximizing the cross-correlation of 1 s book-midpoint changes, compared with unrelated-game placebo pairs (Mann-Whitney, Holm), kickoff window cut. \textbf{Strategies.} A: \val{spec_A_entry_min} min before kickoff, buy the favorite if its price is at least $\theta \in \{\val{spec_A_thetas}\}$, hold to settlement. B: enter when the venue gap $|d| \geq k$ for $m$ seconds, exit when $|d|<$ one cent or after $T$ seconds, $k\in\{\val{spec_B_k_grid_c}\}$ cents, $m\in\{\val{spec_B_m_grid_s}\}$ s, $T\in\{\val{spec_B_T_grid_s}\}$ s. Selection on training only; confidence intervals are game-level bootstraps; Sharpe is mean over standard deviation of daily P\&L (as a share of the capital base), $\times\sqrt{365}$, over every calendar day of the season with zero days kept. \textbf{Multiple testing.} Every setting tried, pre-registered or not, is a row in \texttt{experiments/variants.csv} (\val{count_variants_rows} rows), and the deflated Sharpe ratio \cite{bailey2014} is reported at the pre-registered and the final trial counts.

Kalshi vs Polymarket US: \val{OOS_lead_Polymarket_US_n_qualifying} games, median lag \val{f_OOS_lead_Polymarket_US_detail_median_corrected_lag_s} s, \val{f_OOS_lead_Polymarket_US_detail_share_positive} positive, $p=$ \val{lead_pmus_mw_p} against a Holm level of \val{f_OOS_lead_Polymarket_US_detail_holm_level}: ``\val{f_OOS_lead_Polymarket_US_decision}''. \textbf{Traps.} Trade-price lead-lag on training showed a Kalshi lead that unrelated-game placebos matched; in simulation with no true lead, thin trade prints fake leads while book midpoints do not \cite{lomackinlay1990,hoffmann2013}. The v3 laggard strategy on Polymarket US (not v4) saw a lead only because the quote endpoint was cached (\texttt{max-age} \val{diag_pmus_cache_max_age_s} s): its fills on polled quotes look profitable (\val{laggard_pmus_1s_edge_c} cents), but its fills confirmed by real trades lost \val{f_diag_pmus_partial_run_1p0s_confirmed_mean_c} cents per contract (n \val{diag_pmus_partial_run_1p0s_confirmed_n_fills}). The v3 laggard against polymarket.com (not v4) loses \val{laggard_pmcom_1s_edge_c} cents.

\textbf{v3 Strategy A (favorite-longshot, taker; not v4).} Selected $\theta=\val{f_A_theta}$ on training (return on capital \val{f_A_roc_webull}, \val{A_n_trades} trades; flat plateau). v3 holdout: \val{OOS_A_n_trades} trades in \val{OOS_A_n_games} games, return on capital \val{f_OOS_A_roc_webull} \ci{f_OOS_A_roc_webull_ci_0}{f_OOS_A_roc_webull_ci_1}; all three pre-registered fail conditions trigger. The bias is real in direction (the underdog at the same moment returns \val{f_A_placebo_roc_webull}) but smaller than taker costs. \textbf{v3 A-maker (not v4):} \val{OOS_A_maker_fills} fills in \val{OOS_A_maker_attempts} attempts, return on capital \val{f_OOS_A_maker_roc_webull}: fills came when the market moved against the order (adverse selection), the risk v4 must overcome.

\textbf{v3 Strategy B (cross-venue disagreement; not v4).} Selected $k=\val{f_B_selected_setting_k}$ cents, $m=\val{B_selected_setting_m}$ s, $T=\val{B_selected_setting_T}$ s on training (already negative). v3 holdout: \val{OOS_B_n_trades} trades, \val{f_OOS_B_edge_webull_cents} cents per contract \ci{f_OOS_B_edge_webull_ci_0}{f_OOS_B_edge_webull_ci_1}; gross edge is near zero and B pays a near-fixed round-trip cost on every trade, hence its very negative Sharpe.

\section{Risk management}

v4 quotes \val{spec_qty} contracts per side with an inventory cap of $\pm$\val{v4_cap} contracts per game. Arm B quotes only while the spread is at least \val{v4_armb_c} cents, where the reward per fill was largest on the origin day. Fills count only on strict trade-through, a conservative rule. Settlement and regulatory risk apply (an early-settled and reversed Kalshi market; Nevada enforcement against Kalshi and Polymarket US). Before live trading we would add a daily loss limit, a per-venue exposure cap, a kill switch on feed outages over 60 s and fill reconciliation. All trading is simulated.

\section{Liquidity and capacity}

For a maker the binding constraint is queue position, not size. Queue position on the origin day is unknown, and strict trade-through makes the fill count a lower bound. Deep NFL books with 1 cent spreads may rarely reach a joining quote; thinner college books with wider spreads are where Arm B operates.

\section{Limitations and next steps}

The origin is one day of mostly college games, top of book only. The maker fee was 0 on Oct 3 and may change; test set 2 applies each venue's published schedule at the time. polymarket.com is not available to US persons, so a tradable version needs Polymarket US, whose authenticated order book we have never recorded; the test set 2 recorder still has to be built and running before Oct 8.

\section{Conclusion}

HYPOTHESIS\_v4 is a bet on the other side of the trade, in largely uncharted territory: that a patient maker is paid for supplying liquidity to recreational football flow. On one recorded day it captured \val{mmN_spread_c} cents of spread per contract and lost \val{mmN_adverse_c} cents to adverse selection, net \val{mmN_pnl60_c} \val{mmN_pnl60_ci} cents, concentrated in five games. High risk, high reward: a reason to test, not yet a result. v4 is frozen (\texttt{\val{v4_commit}}) and will be tested once on Oct 8 to 12, and we will report the answer either way.
